# Storm vs stock CRoaring vs patched CRoaring

Generated by `bench/three_way.sh` from `results/three_way.csv`. All three timed in the
same process on the same rows and the same pair set, one untimed warm-up each, repeated
to a 100 ms floor, minimum taken, median of three whole-process runs. Exact
cardinalities; checksums identical across all three on every corpus.

**Storm** is the shipped online policy: density-sorted tiling, per-tile cell selection from
a calibrated cost model, heterogeneity-gated per-pair refinement, O(1) span test. One
policy, chosen from the data at tile-decision time -- not a best-of over policies and not
a per-corpus choice.

**stock Roaring** is CRoaring as shipped, with `run_optimize()`.

**patched Roaring** adds `roaring_bitmap_storm_promote_arrays()`: CRoaring assigns a bitset
container only when a chunk's own cardinality exceeds 4096, which is the *storage* crossover
(`M/e = 65536/16`); the *compute* crossover for a count-only AND is near 64. The patch
promotes array containers above 64 elements to bitsets after `run_optimize()`.

`Ro patch` = stock / patched: what the patch is worth **to Roaring alone**, Storm not involved.

## When selection wins most, and when it degenerates into Roaring

Storm and Roaring select from the same three representation families — Roaring's array,
bitset and run containers are our S, B and R. The difference is granularity: Roaring
chooses per 2^16 chunk with fixed thresholds; we choose per row and per pair from a cost
model. Every regime below follows from that one difference.

**Largest wins, 3.1×–6.5×: a run representation that spans the whole row.** Roaring can only
use a run container *inside* one chunk. Where runs extend across chunk boundaries, our B×R
sees structure Roaring's per-chunk view cannot: `wikileaks-noquotes` **6.47×** (B×R on 39%
of pairs), `dimension_033` **3.08×** (B×R on 70.5%). `usher_sarscov2` at 1.92% is the same
mechanism weaker (B×R 25.5%).

**Largest wins, second mechanism: Roaring runs out of chunks to adapt over.** The msprime
family is a controlled test — same generator, same allele-frequency distribution, only the
universe differs, so only Roaring's chunk count changes:

| corpus | universe | Roaring chunks | vs patched |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `msprime_10k` | 2.0e4 | **1** | **3.77×** |
| `msprime_100k` | 2.0e5 | 4 | 1.74× |
| `msprime_1M` | 2.0e6 | 31 | 1.31× |

At a universe below 2^16 Roaring has a single container and therefore no adaptivity at all,
which is exactly the fixed-representation baseline this work argues against; as chunks
accumulate it regains per-chunk selection and our margin decays monotonically. This is the
clearest evidence that the two methods are the same idea at different granularity.

**Middle, 1.8×–2.7×: sparse graph and IR corpora.** B×S carries 70–99% of pairs. One good
representation per row plus a near-free decision, against Roaring's per-chunk key merge.

**Degeneration, 1.2×–1.7×: when the right answer is a dense bitmap.** `weather_sept_85`
**1.18×** routes 45.9% of pairs to B×B and `census-income` **1.74×** routes 46.1%. When
both operands are dense the answer is an AND plus a popcount, both systems do exactly that,
and there is nothing left to select. This is degeneration in the intended sense: we do not
lose, we converge, because the method has correctly identified that no cheaper
representation exists.

**The span test is parity, not advantage.** `census1881` settles 63.3% of its pairs by the
O(1) span test and still only reaches **1.24×**, because Roaring's chunk-key merge settles
disjoint pairs cheaply too. Skipping empty work is something both systems already do; it is
not where our margin comes from.

## The one corpus we do not win

`dimension_008`, 0.93× against patched Roaring. Not a kernel deficit: R x R applied to every
pair measures **1.05×** against patched Roaring there, so the winning cell exists and is
implemented. The selector routes **0%** of pairs to it.

The cause is in the work function, not the rates. `work_units(RR) = ra + rb` against
`work_units(BR) = min(ra, rb)`: one counts both sides, the other one side. At calibrated
rates 1.99 and 0.86 that prices R x R at least 4.6× above B x R whenever `ra ≈ rb`, so it
can never be selected however fast it is. Five independent attempts to fix this by
changing what is *measured* all failed -- `calibrate_on_rows` in three variants, calibrating
the run cells on run-structured input, and a direct scale on R x R's rate (harmless at 0.4,
and at 0.15 it costs `wikileaks-noquotes` two thirds of its margin). No scalar on either
rate can reconcile quantities that are not commensurable. The fix is to give R x R a unit
that means what B x R's means, which is a change to `work_units`.

## Provenance

Apple M4, P-core L2 16 MiB (read from `hw.perflevel0.l2cachesize`). Between-process spread
on this machine reaches ~30% at these working-set sizes, so single-run differences below
~15% are not resolvable by comparing sweeps; `bench/ab.sh` provides paired A/B for those.
Ranges in `results/vs_roaring.csv`.

**Known defect in `bench/three_way.sh`**: it emits the display line and the CSV line from one
call and splits them with `read`, which splits on all whitespace, so CSV rows are written
with display text prepended. This run was recovered by regex and `enwiki-categorylinks`,
dropped by the recovery, was re-measured separately. Fix before the next run.
