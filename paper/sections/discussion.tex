\section*{Discussion}

This paper has argued, and measured, that in set-intersection cardinality, choosing which
representation pairing to evaluate --- cheaply, per operand pair or per tile, from properties
measured online rather than modelled in advance --- dominates the return available from making any
single kernel faster, across the density range in which anything can be skipped at all. Across
the asymmetric cells a strategy built around avoiding work wins essentially every measurement taken,
and the resulting selector outperforms a competitively tuned Roaring baseline on every real corpus
tested, with no single representation pairing winning enough of that corpus set to make the pairing
matrix itself unnecessary. The shape of this contribution is the same shape that made Roaring's own
contribution land: array containers, bitset containers, run containers and SIMD intersection were each
individually known before Roaring existed, and what made the format matter in practice was the
composition of known parts into one adaptively dispatched system, evaluated and deployed as a whole
rather than argued for container by container \cite{chambi2016roaring,lemire2018roaring}. The claim
advanced here is structured identically: a selector over a wider representation-pairing matrix, gated
on measured rather than modelled properties, is the contribution, not any single cell or mechanism
inside it.

Conceding this plainly matters. Roaring already performs adaptive, per-chunk representation choice,
and the contribution reported here is not the general idea that representation should be chosen rather
than fixed. What differs is narrower, and more precise. The representation set considered here is
wider than Roaring's three per-chunk containers --- it adds a fill-compressed word stream and an
explicit complement strategy for the dense tail --- and the cardinality-only setting admits a shortcut
that a materializing container format cannot take without giving up the ability to produce the
intersection itself: a rank index answers a run's entire contribution in constant time, independent of
its length (Methods, ``Zone-map and rank-index construction''). The cost of \emph{deciding} is also
treated here as a first-order quantity rather than an implementation detail. Roaring's per-chunk
container choice is made once at ingest and never revisited, so it never has to be priced; a
selector that chooses per operand pair and per region does, and that price scales inversely with how
large the operation being decided about is --- negligible for one operation between two large sets,
a substantial fraction of runtime when many small operations each pay for their own decision, which
is what forces hoisting and probe-and-commit (Fig.~\ref{fig:selection-cost}a). The selector's own
metadata is treated as part of the representation, built at ingest and amortised over every
operation the set ever participates in, which is the same assumption Roaring's container choice
already makes. The clearest concrete instance of a fixed per-chunk threshold no longer matching global density is
read directly from Roaring's own container statistics (Table~\ref{tab:census}); it is evidence about a
materialization-oriented format applied to a cardinality-only query, not evidence of poor engineering,
and it does not diminish what Roaring achieved or how widely it is deployed.

The mechanism behind that migration is worth stating on its own terms. Across the asymmetric
cells, the kernels that win are consistently the ones that decide not to touch data --- a rank lookup
that answers an entire run without reading its bits, a zone map that proves two rows disjoint from a
fraction of their footprint, a search strategy that steps over the dense side rather than scanning it
--- and not the ones that process the same bytes with a wider instruction
(Fig.~\ref{fig:strategy-decay}a). The two strategies
also respond to the memory hierarchy in opposite directions, and this is where the finding reaches past
a single measurement. Vector throughput is a per-cycle constant that a wider core keeps buying more of,
so its advantage is realized fully only while data stays close enough to be fed at that rate; work
avoidance instead removes a count of memory-hierarchy crossings, a cost that widening a core does not
by itself reduce. \spec{If core width and the ratio of cache capacity to DRAM bandwidth continue to
diverge the way recent processor generations suggest, the boundary this paper locates should move
further toward avoidance rather than settle where it is measured here --- a prediction about hardware
not yet tested, not a claim about the hardware tested in this study.}

Underneath every mechanism here is one way of accounting for structure, and it is worth separating
from the artifact that carries it, because it is the part that travels. Choosing a representation,
consulting a zone map, composing the two, pruning by prefix --- each is a bet that the data deviates
from randomness, and each admits a closed-form null model saying what the bet is worth if it does
not. What makes the accounting useful is that in every case the null is the \emph{worst} case rather
than the expected one: placing elements independently at a given density maximises the number of
maximal runs a set has, maximises the fraction of bins it occupies, and maximises the chance that
two prefixes collide. Real data can therefore deviate only in the mechanism's favour, and the size
of that deviation is exactly what the mechanism converts into avoided work. The null model is a
floor, not a prediction; structure is the resource, and the mechanisms are the machinery that spends
it. Two impossibilities follow, and together they are this paper's formal argument for measuring
rather than modelling. A representation fixed at ingest cannot exploit the deviation, having
committed before the deviation is knowable. A cost model whose inputs are densities or cardinalities
cannot exploit it either, and not for want of sophistication: density is the very statistic the null
is built from, so any function of it returns one value for every set of that density, while the
descriptor length of the arrangement-sensitive representations ranges over orders of magnitude at
fixed density. Both arguments rule out families of selectors without naming a winner among the
survivors; which survivor is best is settled here by measurement, and that a mis-specified model can
land behind making no choice at all (Fig.~\ref{fig:selection-cost}b) is the empirical form of the
same point. The quantity that decides is cheaper to measure than to predict.

Seen through that accounting the mechanisms order themselves, and the order is by what a caller
gives up rather than by what anything costs. Representation choice and the zone map give up nothing:
the same question returns the same answer and only the work changes, and they carry the results
above without help. Prefix filtering and the size bound give up generality in exchange for far more,
and are available only once a caller says which pairs they actually want --- a size bound is the
extreme of this, discarding a pair from its two cardinalities without reading either operand, and it
is the one mechanism here whose leverage grows as a corpus's set sizes spread further apart. That
bound is not ours: it is the Swamidass--Baldi pruning result \cite{swamidass2007bounds} and the size
filter of the similarity-join literature \cite{bayardo2007allpairs,xiao2008ppjoin}, and this paper
contributes its placement rather than the bound. Batch amortisation is different again --- the
contract is unchanged, but it needs many operations, because what it removes is repetition: metadata
built at ingest and reused for the life of a set, a decision hoisted so one serves many pairs, a
probe whose verdict is reused, operands loaded once and paired against many. That is a regime rather
than a premise, it requires no particular set of pairs, and it is where this paper's selection
measurements were taken; the single-operation regime is argued from the cost of an $O(1)$ decision
and has not been measured to the same standard. Two cautions travel with the ordering. Placing a
mechanism at a coarser granularity does not by itself make its saving multiply with the others: the
size bound and everything below it all read cardinality, and sharing that statistic costs the
composition a factor that grows with the spread of set sizes, even though the mechanisms act at
different granularities. And the narrowing mechanisms are opt-in throughout --- nothing in the
exact path depends on them, and a reader should not take the headline results as resting on a
smaller question than the one they appear to answer.

Stated that way the result also bounds itself, in two directions worth naming because the framing
invites over-reading. Every mechanism here carries a two-sided liability. A summary must be declined
when the operands are too dense, because every bin is occupied and it proves nothing; and equally
when they are too sparse, because a summary is sized by the universe while a compressed
representation is sized by the data, so below a crossing density consulting it costs more than the
entire computation it was meant to shorten --- the same phenomenon that appears in the storage
figures as universe-proportional selector metadata. And around density one half the cheapest
descriptor is no smaller than the bitmap: cardinalities determine least there, nothing is available
to skip, and the fixed-cost kernel is simply the correct one. That band is not a shortfall to be
closed but the domain of the other optimization axis, and it is an axis the field has already
carried a long way. Where work is unavoidable, what remains is to execute it at the hardware's
throughput limit \cite{mula2018popcount}, and this paper's own dense cell is fast for precisely that
reason rather than in spite of the argument made here. The claim is therefore narrower and more
useful than ``avoid work'': avoid the work the analysis says can be avoided, accelerate the work it
says cannot, and treat the boundary between them as the thing worth knowing. The analytic curves
that locate that boundary are models of \emph{work}, not of time, and the gap between the two is
itself instructive --- the runtime penalty for consulting a summary that removes nothing is far
larger than its word count, because it is paid in the memory system rather than in the operation
count, which is a third and independent reason to gate on measurement.

The property doing the work throughout this paper is heterogeneity, not sparsity. A corpus in which
every row sits at the same density has nothing for a pairing matrix to exploit, regardless of how
sparse or dense that shared density is; what representation selection is paid for is variance in
density across rows that share one universe and one cost model, of which sparsity is only the more
familiar half --- the dense tail rewards exactly the same reasoning, mirrored around the complement.
\spec{Read this way, the same argument should transfer to any workload that mixes elements of very
different density under a shared representation and a shared cost model: sparse linear-algebra format
selection choosing among dense, compressed-sparse-row and coordinate tiles, or a columnar query engine
deciding which blocks a predicate can skip without scanning, are two settings where the condition
plausibly holds.} Neither has been tested here, and the claim is offered as where the mechanism should
generalize, not as a result obtained in either domain.

Several things remain open, stated plainly. The selector is cheap relative to the saving it protects,
but it is not close to optimal: nothing measured closes the residual gap to a bucket oracle that
already knows the winning cell (Fig.~\ref{fig:selection-cost}b), and closing that margin is
unfinished work rather than a property of the
method. Every measurement reported here is single-threaded by construction; the real-corpus comparison
ran on \nm{number and identity of microarchitectures used for the real-corpus comparison} while the
synthetic cross-architecture comparison spans \nm{count of microarchitectures in the synthetic
cross-architecture comparison} microarchitectures; and the tile pipeline has so far
been evaluated on within-tile pairs only, with the cross-tile case and the transpose-build cost left
for the end-to-end evaluation that follows this paper. Most directly: the pairing matrix, the selection
gate and the tile pipeline described here are not currently reachable through the project's public C
ABI. This paper reports a systematic measurement of the underlying mechanism, not a released tool, and
the ABI and packaging work needed to close that gap is sequenced immediately after it, with a
genome-wide linkage-disequilibrium application, Tomahawk \cite{tomahawk_github}, as the eventual, and
strictly later, use of the mechanism rather than its motivation. What should outlast any single ratio
in this paper is the map itself --- which representation pairing wins where, and why --- because that
map, and the boundary between avoiding work and accelerating it that it makes visible, is transferable
independent of the number attached to any one cell.
